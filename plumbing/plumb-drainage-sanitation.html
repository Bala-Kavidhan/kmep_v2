Overview

What is Drainage & Sanitation Systems?

A drainage system carries used water and waste away from the building, and storm water away from roofs and paved areas. It must do this quietly, without blockage and without letting sewer gas into occupied spaces.

Inside the building, soil pipes carry waste from toilets, and waste pipes carry water from basins, showers and kitchens. Each fixture has a trap, a bend that holds a water seal. Vent pipes bring in air so the water seals are not sucked out and the flow is smooth. Outside, the pipes run to inspection chambers and manholes, and then to the sewer, a septic tank or a sewage treatment plant.

Storm water is drained separately from sewage. Roof gutters, downpipes and site drains are sized for the heaviest local rainfall, so heavy rain does not flood the building.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Designed to NBC Part 9, with IS 1742 for building drainage.
  • Pipes sized by fixture units, with a larger diameter for toilets and a minimum size for each branch.
  • Gradients steeper for small pipes and flatter for large pipes, as set by the code.
  • A water seal in every trap to block sewer gas.
  • Vent pipes terminated above the roof, away from openings.
  • Inspection chambers or manholes at changes in direction, junctions and at regular intervals on long runs.
  • Separate systems for sewage and storm water.
  • Rainwater sized from local rainfall intensity and roof area.
  • Sump pumps with duty and standby for basements, with high-level alarms.
  • Grease traps on kitchen drains and oil or silt interceptors where needed.
  • Testing by water, air or smoke before concealment.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a trap?

A bend in the pipe that holds water and blocks sewer gas.

Why separate storm water from sewage?

To avoid overloading the sewer and treatment plant, and so rain can be reused or recharged.

What is an invert level?

The level of the bottom of a drain pipe. It sets the slope and connection depth.

Do basements need a pump?

If the basement is below the sewer level, yes. A sump pit with pumps lifts the water out.
