Overview

What is Sewage Treatment Plants (STP)?

A sewage treatment plant (STP) cleans wastewater from toilets, kitchens and bathrooms so it can be safely discharged or reused. Large residential, commercial and institutional buildings are generally required to have one, because untreated sewage pollutes water and spreads disease.

Most STPs use a biological process. Bacteria are given oxygen and consume the organic matter in the sewage. The water then settles to separate the sludge, is filtered, and is disinfected. The treated water meets limits for pollutants such as BOD and suspended solids, and can be used for flushing, gardening, cooling towers and washing.

The right process depends on flow, available space, noise and odour limits, and the quality required. Common choices are MBBR, SBR, extended aeration and MBR.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Sewage flow taken as a large fraction of water supplied, commonly about 80 percent.
  • Treated water quality to the limits set by the CPCB and the State Pollution Control Board, such as BOD and suspended solids.
  • Equalisation tank to even out flow and load.
  • Blowers and diffusers sized for the oxygen demand.
  • Clarifier sized for the settling rate.
  • Pressure sand and activated carbon filters, followed by chlorine or UV disinfection.
  • Treated water storage and separate pipework for flushing and landscaping.
  • Sludge drying beds or mechanical dewatering.
  • Covers, vents and odour control where the plant is near occupied areas.
  • Online or regular lab testing of inlet and outlet water.
  • Standby blowers and pumps for continuity.

Common STP processes

ProcessNotes
Extended aerationSimple and reliable. Needs more space.
MBBRCompact, uses plastic media for bacteria, stable under load changes.
SBRTreatment in batches in one tank, good control, compact.
MBRMembrane filtration gives high-quality water in small space. Higher cost.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is BOD?

Biochemical oxygen demand: how much oxygen bacteria need to break down the organic matter in water. It is a key measure of pollution.

Where can treated water be used?

Flushing, landscaping, cooling tower make-up and washing, but not for drinking.

Is an STP mandatory?

Many large developments are required to have one under local rules. We check the requirement for your project.

Who operates the STP?

A trained operator, with regular maintenance and testing.
