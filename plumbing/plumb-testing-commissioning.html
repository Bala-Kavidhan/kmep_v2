Overview

What is Testing, Commissioning & Performance Validation?

Testing and commissioning proves that a plumbing installation holds pressure, drains correctly, delivers the right flow and pressure, and is safe to use. Most plumbing is hidden in walls, floors and shafts, so faults must be found and fixed before it is covered up.

Tests are done in stages. Supply pipes are pressure tested. Drainage is checked by water, air or smoke. Tanks are leak tested. Pumps are run and their flow and pressure measured. New pipework is flushed and disinfected, and water quality is tested. Treatment plants and rainwater systems are run through trial operation.

At the end, results, drawings and manuals are handed over so the owner can operate and maintain the system.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Supply lines tested hydrostatically at a pressure above working pressure, commonly 1.5 times, held for the duration in the specification, with no pressure drop.
  • Drainage tested before concealment using water fill, air or smoke.
  • Tanks filled and observed for leaks for the period specified.
  • Pumps tested for flow, head, current, vibration and noise.
  • Disinfection with chlorine solution followed by flushing until residual levels are acceptable.
  • Water tested for potability against IS 10500.
  • Flow and pressure measured at the farthest and highest fixtures.
  • Hot water temperature checked, and mixing valves set.
  • STP outlet samples tested in a laboratory.
  • Test certificates signed by the client or consultant.
  • As-built drawings and O&M manuals handed over.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Why test before closing walls?

Repairing a hidden leak later means breaking finished work.

What is chlorination?

Filling new pipes with a chlorine solution for a set time to kill bacteria, then flushing.

How long is a pressure test held?

As per the specification. It is typically several hours with no drop.

Do you provide test reports?

Yes, signed records for each test.
