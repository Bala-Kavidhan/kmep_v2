Overview

What is Water Supply & Distribution?

A water supply system brings water from the municipal main, a borewell or a tanker into the building, stores it, raises it to the required level and delivers it to every fixture at a usable pressure. It is a chain of tanks, pumps, pipes, valves and meters, and the weakest link sets the performance of the whole system.

Most buildings use a sump at the ground level and an overhead tank on the roof. Water is pumped up to the overhead tank and flows down by gravity, or boosters feed the risers directly at constant pressure. In tall buildings the system is divided into zones so that no floor sees too much or too little pressure.

Hot water is produced centrally by solar heaters, heat pumps or boilers, or locally by electric heaters, and circulated so users do not wait for it. Water quality matters too: tanks are protected, filtered and disinfected.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Tanks with covers, vents with insect mesh, overflow and scour pipes, and access for cleaning.
  • Separate tanks for domestic and fire water.
  • Pumps with duty and standby sets and automatic level control.
  • Constant-pressure booster with VFD for direct supply systems.
  • Pressure zones so fixture pressure stays within the recommended range.
  • Hot water stored at a temperature that limits bacterial growth, with mixing valves at the point of use to avoid scalding.
  • Pipe insulation on hot water lines.
  • Isolation valves, non-return valves and strainers in the right places.
  • Water meters on incoming lines and, where useful, on floors or tenants.
  • Hydrostatic test and disinfection before commissioning, and water quality checked against IS 10500.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Overhead tank or booster pump?

Overhead tanks are simple and give gravity supply. Boosters give constant pressure without a rooftop tank. Many buildings use a combination.

Why disinfect new pipes?

Construction leaves dirt and bacteria. Chlorine disinfection and flushing make the system safe.

Why insulate hot water pipes?

To save heat and to deliver hot water faster.

What is a dead leg?

A long unused branch where water stands. It wastes water and can harbour bacteria.
