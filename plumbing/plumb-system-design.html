Overview

What is Plumbing System Design & Engineering?

Plumbing system design plans how water reaches every tap and how waste leaves every fixture. It covers cold and hot water supply, storage and pumping, drainage, venting, storm water and the fixtures themselves, and it must work correctly on every floor at every hour of the day.

Design starts from demand. The number of people and fixtures, and the type of building, give the daily water requirement and the peak flow. Tanks, pumps and pipe sizes follow. On the drainage side, fixture units set the size and slope of soil, waste and vent pipes so that waste flows away freely and foul air never enters occupied spaces.

Plumbing also has to fit. Shafts, ceilings, slab drops and basement levels are shared with HVAC, electrical and structure, so coordination is part of the design from the start.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Codes: NBC Part 9 on plumbing services, with IS 1172, IS 2065 for water supply in buildings and IS 1742 for building drainage, and local authority rules.
  • Water demand taken per person as per NBC, for example about 135 litres per day in residences and about 45 litres in offices, with roughly one third of residential demand used for flushing.
  • Storage sized from daily demand, split between underground and overhead tanks, with fire water stored separately.
  • Pipe velocity kept moderate, commonly up to about 2 to 3 m/s, to limit noise and wear.
  • Residual pressure at fixtures generally about 0.5 to 1 bar, higher for flush valves, with pressure-reducing valves in tall buildings so the lower floors are not over-pressured.
  • Drainage designed with fixture units, correct gradients, traps with water seals and vent pipes.
  • Separate systems for soil and waste, storm water, and treated water where reuse is planned.
  • Materials chosen for the service: CPVC, PPR, GI or copper for supply, and UPVC or cast iron for drainage.
  • Isolation valves at every branch and riser for easy maintenance.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a fixture unit?

A number assigned to each fixture based on its water use and discharge, used to size pipes.

Why are vent pipes needed?

They let air into the drainage system so water flows freely and traps keep their seal.

What is a pressure-reducing valve for?

It lowers high pressure in lower floors of tall buildings to protect fixtures and avoid noise.

Why is fire water kept separate?

It must always be available, so it is stored apart from domestic water.
