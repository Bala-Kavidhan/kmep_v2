Overview

What is Rainwater Harvesting & Reuse?

Rainwater harvesting captures the rain that falls on roofs and paved areas and either stores it for use or lets it soak into the ground to recharge groundwater. In many cities it reduces the water drawn from the mains and from borewells, and it reduces flooding by slowing runoff.

There are two main approaches. In storage systems, water from the roof is filtered and stored in a tank for use in flushing, gardening or cooling towers. In recharge systems, water is led through a filter into a recharge pit, trench or well that returns it to the aquifer. Many buildings use both, with the tank filling first and the overflow going to recharge.

Rainwater harvesting is required for buildings by state rules in Tamil Nadu, so design should follow the current local requirements.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Harvestable volume equals the catchment area, times rainfall, times the runoff coefficient. Roofs usually have a high coefficient and paved ground a lower one.
  • Local rainfall data from the India Meteorological Department.
  • Gutters and downpipes sized for peak rainfall intensity.
  • First-flush diverter to discard the initial dirty runoff.
  • Filter media such as gravel, sand and charcoal in layers.
  • Recharge structures sized for the volume of a design storm and the soil infiltration rate.
  • Recharge water kept clear of sewage and contaminants.
  • Storage tanks covered, vented and with overflow to recharge.
  • Reuse water kept in a separate marked pipe system, not for drinking unless treated.
  • Maintenance plan: clean gutters and filters before the monsoon.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is rainwater harvesting compulsory?

In Tamil Nadu, state rules require it for buildings. We check the current requirements for each project.

Can harvested rainwater be drunk?

Not without proper treatment and testing. It is normally used for flushing, gardening and washing.

What is a first-flush diverter?

A device that discards the first part of the rainfall, which carries the most dirt from the roof.

How often should the system be maintained?

Clean gutters and filters before each monsoon, and check recharge structures for silt.
