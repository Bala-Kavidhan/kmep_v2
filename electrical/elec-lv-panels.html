Overview

What is LV Panels?

Low-voltage (LV) panels are the enclosures that receive power and divide it among the loads in a building. They contain busbars, circuit breakers, contactors, relays, meters and wiring, all housed in a steel cabinet that keeps people safe and equipment protected.

Different panels do different jobs. A Power Control Centre (PCC) or main LV panel receives power from the transformer and feeds the building. A Motor Control Centre (MCC) controls and protects motors such as pumps and fans. Distribution boards (DBs) feed lighting and small power on each floor. Specialised panels include changeover panels, capacitor panels and control panels.

Because everything else depends on them, panels must be well designed, built to standard and tested before they leave the workshop.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Assemblies built and tested to IS/IEC 61439 or IS 8623, with devices to IS/IEC 60947.
  • Busbars of copper or aluminium sized for rated current and short-circuit withstand.
  • Form of separation chosen to limit the effect of a fault and allow safe maintenance.
  • Degree of protection by IP rating, such as IP42 indoors and higher for dust or water.
  • Short-circuit rating matched to the fault level at the panel.
  • Breakers selected and set to discriminate with upstream and downstream devices.
  • Sheet steel of adequate thickness, treated and powder-coated.
  • Clear labelling, wire numbering and mimic diagrams.
  • Space for future feeders and cable entry.
  • Routine tests at the workshop, with a test certificate.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between PCC and MCC?

A PCC distributes power to feeders. An MCC controls and protects motors.

What is a form of separation?

The way busbars, functional units and terminals are separated inside the panel, which limits the spread of faults.

What does IP rating mean?

Ingress Protection: how well the enclosure resists dust and water.

Why are routine tests done at the workshop?

To find faults before the panel is installed and energised.
