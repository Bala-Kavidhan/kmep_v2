DG & UPS Backup

Generator and UPS systems that keep critical loads running through a mains failure.

Overview

What is DG & UPS Backup?

Backup power keeps essential services running when the mains supply fails. Two systems usually work together. A diesel generator (DG) set supplies power for as long as it has fuel, but takes several seconds to start. An Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) with batteries supplies power instantly for a short time, bridging the gap until the generator is ready.

The design first decides which loads must be backed up: lifts, fire pumps, emergency lighting, IT and critical equipment, and perhaps the whole building. The generator is then sized for the running load plus the starting current of motors. An Auto Mains Failure (AMF) panel detects the failure, starts the generator and transfers the load automatically.

Installation affects performance as much as sizing: ventilation, exhaust, fuel storage, noise control, earthing and cable routes must all be designed with the generator.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Generator sizing for running load plus starting kVA of the largest motor, with a margin.
  • Engine and alternator standards such as ISO 8528, and CPCB norms for emissions and noise.
  • Acoustic enclosure or room treatment to meet noise limits.
  • Exhaust routed safely with a stack of adequate height.
  • Day fuel tank with bulk storage, as per safety rules.
  • AMF panel with start delay, transfer delay and interlock against back-feeding.
  • Online double-conversion UPS to IEC 62040 for critical loads.
  • Battery type and autonomy chosen for the application, such as 15 to 30 minutes for IT loads, and N+1 redundancy for critical sites.
  • Earthing and neutral arrangement coordinated with the main system.
  • Regular exercise and load testing plan.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Why do I need both a DG and a UPS?

The UPS covers the few seconds before the generator starts, and the generator provides long-term power.

What is an AMF panel?

Auto Mains Failure panel: it detects mains failure, starts the generator and transfers the load.

How is generator size decided?

By the running load and the starting current of the largest motors, with margin.

How often should the DG be tested?

Run it on load regularly. The schedule is set in the maintenance plan.
