Overview

What is BMS, Controls & Automation Integration?

A Building Management System (BMS) is a computer-based system that monitors and controls a building's services from one place. It collects data from sensors and equipment, applies control logic, and lets operators see and adjust the building through a graphical interface.

Typical systems connected include air-handling units, chillers, pumps, fans, lighting, energy meters, generators and water tanks. The BMS can start and stop equipment on schedules, adjust setpoints, raise alarms when something goes wrong, and log data to show how the building performs.

Integration is the hard part. Equipment from different manufacturers speaks different languages, so the BMS uses open protocols such as BACnet and Modbus, or gateways, to bring everything into one system.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Three-level architecture: field devices, controllers and a supervisory server.
  • Open protocols such as BACnet and Modbus to avoid dependence on one maker.
  • Points list with analogue and digital inputs and outputs for every system.
  • Written sequence of operation for each system, including fault behaviour.
  • Schedules, setpoint control, optimum start and demand limiting.
  • Alarm priorities and notification to operators.
  • Trend logs with enough history to analyse performance.
  • Energy sub-metering by floor, tenant or system.
  • Fire alarm is typically monitored only, not controlled, by the BMS, as per code.
  • Network security, access control and backups for the server.
  • Training and documentation for operators.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between BMS and SCADA?

A BMS is aimed at building services. SCADA is a broader term used for industrial and utility supervision. They work in similar ways.

What is BACnet?

An open communication standard designed for building automation.

Can a BMS control the fire alarm?

Normally the BMS only monitors fire alarm status. Fire systems are kept independent.

Can an old building be integrated?

Often yes, using gateways and new controllers. We survey the existing systems first.
