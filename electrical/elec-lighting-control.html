Overview

What is Lighting Design & Control?

Good lighting lets people see well, work comfortably and feel at ease, while using as little energy as possible. Lighting design chooses the fittings, positions them and controls them to meet these goals.

Design starts from the task. Offices need even, glare-free light on desks and screens. Corridors need less. Retail and hospitality use accent lighting to set mood. For each space the designer sets the target illuminance in lux, the colour temperature, the colour rendering and the glare limits, then calculates the layout to achieve them.

Controls then reduce energy use. Occupancy sensors switch lights off in empty rooms, daylight sensors dim lights near windows, and scheduling and scene control match light to the way each space is used.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Illuminance levels based on IS 3646 and NBC Part 8 guidance, set per space type.
  • Lighting simulation such as DIALux used to check lux, uniformity and glare.
  • Colour temperature typically 3000 K to 4000 K for interiors, with good colour rendering of CRI 80 or above.
  • Glare control by luminaire type and positioning, for example using UGR limits in offices.
  • Lighting power density kept within ECBC or project energy targets.
  • Maintenance factor included so light levels stay adequate over time.
  • Controls using DALI (IEC 62386), 0-10 V or relays, with sensors and scheduling.
  • Emergency lighting with battery backup to light escape routes, with autonomy as required by code.
  • Surge protection and quality LED drivers for long life.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a lux?

The unit of illuminance: the light falling on a surface.

What is DALI?

Digital Addressable Lighting Interface, a standard for controlling and dimming individual luminaires.

Do LEDs need special drivers?

Yes. The driver quality affects life, flicker and efficiency.

Do you design emergency lighting?

Yes, including exit signs and escape route lighting.
