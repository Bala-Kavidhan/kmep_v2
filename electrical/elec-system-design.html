Overview

What is Electrical System Design & Load Planning?

Electrical system design decides how power enters a building, is divided among its loads and is protected. It begins with a load list and ends with drawings, calculations and specifications that a contractor can build from and an inspector can approve.

The starting point is the connected load: every light, fan, air-conditioner, pump, lift and socket. Because not everything runs at once, a demand factor and diversity factor are applied to get the maximum demand, which sizes the incoming supply, transformer and generator. From there the design works down through main panels, sub-panels and distribution boards, sizing each cable and protective device.

Good design also looks forward. Spare capacity for growth, power factor correction, harmonics, earthing and lightning protection are planned from the start, because adding them later is costly.

Why It Matters

What goes wrong without it

Undersized supply

Overloaded cables, nuisance trips and overheating.

Oversized equipment

Higher capital cost and poor transformer efficiency.

Poor protection coordination

A small fault trips the whole building.

Our Process

How we do it, step by step

1

Collect requirements

Building use, floor areas, equipment lists and client needs. Utility supply conditions.

2

Prepare the load schedule

List every load with rating, quantity, and load type such as lighting, power, HVAC or critical.

3

Calculate maximum demand

Apply demand and diversity factors to find realistic peak load, then size transformer and standby power.

4

Draw the single-line diagram

Show incoming supply, panels, feeders, protection and metering in one diagram.

5

Size cables and protection

Select cable size by current, voltage drop and short-circuit withstand, and choose breakers and settings.

6

Study faults and coordination

Short-circuit levels and discrimination between upstream and downstream devices.

7

Design earthing and lightning protection

Earth electrodes, bonding and, where required, a lightning protection system.

8

Document

Drawings, calculations, specifications, schedules and BOQ.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Codes and standards: National Electrical Code of India, IS 732 for wiring, NBC Part 8 for building services, and the CEA safety regulations.
  • Voltage levels: HT supply, step-down to 415 V three-phase, and 230 V single-phase for final circuits.
  • Maximum demand found with demand and diversity factors, not by adding connected loads.
  • Transformer and generator sizing with margin for motor starting and future load.
  • Cable sizing checked for current-carrying capacity with derating, voltage drop and short-circuit withstand.
  • Voltage drop kept within the limits set by code, commonly about 3 to 5 percent overall.
  • Power factor correction to keep power factor high and avoid utility penalties.
  • Harmonics considered where there are many drives, UPS units and LED drivers.
  • Earthing to IS 3043 and lightning protection to IS/IEC 62305 where the risk assessment requires it.
  • Spare capacity provided in panels and risers for growth.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is maximum demand?

The highest load the building actually draws at once. It is lower than the sum of all connected loads.

What is diversity?

Not all loads operate at the same time, so the combined peak is lower than the total connected load.

Why is power factor important?

Low power factor increases current and losses, and utilities may charge penalties.

How much spare capacity should be allowed?

This depends on the building and growth plans. We discuss it during design.
