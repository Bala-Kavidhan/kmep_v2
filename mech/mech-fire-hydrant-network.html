Overview

What is Fire Hydrant Network?

A fire hydrant system is a permanent water supply network that lets occupants and the fire service fight a fire with hoses. It consists of a dedicated fire water tank, fire pumps, a pipe network, and outlets in the form of hydrants, hose reels and landing valves.

Inside buildings, wet risers run vertically through the structure with a landing valve on each floor. Outside, yard hydrants are laid around the building so fire engines and hoses can reach every side. Fire brigade inlets let the fire service pump water into the system from outside.

Requirements are set by the National Building Code and the local fire authority, and depend on building height, occupancy and area. The system must be reliable above all else: it sits idle for years and must work perfectly the moment it is needed.

Why It Matters

What goes wrong without it

Insufficient water

An undersized tank or pump cannot sustain a fire fight.

Low pressure

Hydrants at the top or far end cannot deliver enough flow.

Failure on demand

Corroded, untested or locked-shut valves stop the system working.

Non-compliance

The building may fail the fire authority's inspection.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Designed as per the National Building Code of India and applicable IS standards such as IS 3844 for internal hydrants and IS 13039 for external hydrants, with the local fire authority's requirements.
  • Minimum residual pressure and flow at the most remote outlet, as set by code for the building class.
  • Pipework of suitable pressure class, painted in fire-red as per colour code, with supports and anchors.
  • Landing valves, hoses, branch pipes and hose reels of approved make and standard.
  • Pumps with automatic start on pressure drop, and a test line to check performance.
  • Sectional isolation valves with tamper protection so a repair does not shut the whole system.
  • Hydrostatic test at a pressure above working pressure for the duration the code requires.
  • Annual and periodic inspection and maintenance plan.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a wet riser?

A vertical pipe permanently filled with water, with landing valves on each floor for firefighting hoses.

Why is there a jockey pump?

It keeps the line pressurised and covers small leaks, so the main pump only starts when a hydrant is actually opened.

Must fire water be separate from domestic water?

Fire water storage is kept dedicated so it is always available.

How often should the system be tested?

Pumps are run regularly and the system inspected on a schedule set by code and the fire authority.
