Overview

What is Fire Sprinkler Systems?

An automatic fire sprinkler system controls or extinguishes a fire without anyone operating it. Sprinklers are fitted to a pipe network that is kept charged with water. When heat from a fire reaches a sprinkler, it opens and sprays water over the area below, and a flow switch raises the alarm.

The design depends on the hazard class of the building. Offices, hotels and hospitals are light hazard. Warehouses and process areas may be ordinary or extra high hazard and need more water over a larger area. The designer chooses sprinkler type, spacing, pipe layout and pump supply to deliver the required density.

Design follows the National Building Code and standards such as IS 15105 or NFPA 13, with the approval of the local fire authority.

Why It Matters

What goes wrong without it

Insufficient water density

Fire spreads because too little water reaches it.

Wrong sprinkler choice

Wrong temperature rating or response type can cause false or late operation.

Obstructed spray

Ducts, beams and ceilings block water distribution.

Unreliable supply

A weak pump or tank cannot sustain the system.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Designed as per IS 15105, NFPA 13 or the standard required by the project, and the National Building Code.
  • Sprinkler bulb colour shows operating temperature, for example red at 68°C is common for ordinary ceilings.
  • Quick-response or standard-response sprinklers chosen by occupancy.
  • Design density in mm per minute over a design area set by hazard class.
  • Maximum coverage area and spacing per sprinkler set by the standard.
  • Pipe sizing by hydraulic calculation, with velocity limits.
  • Alarm check valve, flow switch, tamper switch, pressure gauge and test and drain valve for each zone.
  • Sprinkler water supply from a dedicated pump and tank, with duration as required by the hazard.
  • Hydrostatic testing at the pressure and duration set by the standard.

Sprinkler system types

TypeUsed where
Wet pipeMost buildings. Pipes always hold water, so response is fastest.
Dry pipeAreas that may freeze. Pipes hold air, water enters when a sprinkler opens.
Pre-actionSensitive areas such as data rooms. Needs both detection and sprinkler operation.
DelugeHigh-hazard areas. All sprinklers are open and discharge when the system is triggered.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do all sprinklers open when one activates?

No. Only the sprinklers whose bulbs reach their rated temperature open.

What is a flow switch?

A device that detects water movement in the pipe and sends an alarm signal.

Can sprinklers cause accidental discharge?

Very rarely. Each sprinkler needs enough heat to open.

Are sprinklers and hydrants the same system?

No. Hydrants are for manual firefighting. Sprinklers act automatically. Both may be required.
