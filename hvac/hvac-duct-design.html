Overview

What is Duct Design?

Ducts carry conditioned, fresh and exhaust air between equipment and the spaces served. How they are sized and routed decides whether a room is quiet or noisy, comfortable or drafty, and whether the fan uses little or a lot of power.

Duct design starts from the airflow each room needs, taken from the heat load. The duct network is then sized so air moves at controlled velocity with low friction loss. Common methods are equal friction, static regain and the T-method, chosen by system size and sensitivity.

Good design is also about coordination. Ducts compete with beams, pipes, cable trays and sprinkler lines for ceiling space. Routing is resolved on paper so that site crews do not have to improvise.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Velocity guide for comfort systems: main ducts roughly 5 to 8 m/s, branches 3 to 5 m/s, terminal runouts 2 to 3 m/s in quiet spaces.
  • Friction rate: typically about 0.8 to 1.2 Pa per metre for low-velocity systems.
  • Aspect ratio: kept near 3:1, with 4:1 as a practical limit.
  • Material and fabrication: galvanised sheet steel to IS 655 and SMACNA practice, with gauge set by duct size and pressure class.
  • Insulation: closed-cell elastomeric or glass wool, with cladding where exposed.
  • Fire dampers where ducts cross fire-rated walls and floors.
  • Noise target: typically NC 35 to 40 for offices, lower for studios and bedrooms.
  • Sealing and supports as per SMACNA to limit leakage and vibration.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is static pressure?

The resistance a fan must overcome to push air through ducts, fittings, coils and filters.

Why do ducts whistle?

Usually excessive velocity at bends, dampers or grilles, or poorly fitted components.

Is round duct better than rectangular?

Round ducts have lower friction and leakage. Rectangular ducts fit tighter ceiling heights. The choice depends on space.

Do ducts need insulation?

Yes for cooled air, to stop heat gain and condensation.
