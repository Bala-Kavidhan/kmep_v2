Overview

What is Heat Load Calculation?

Heat load calculation is the process of working out how much heat must be removed from (or added to) every space to hold the required temperature and humidity. It is the first engineering step in any HVAC project, and every later decision, from tonnage to duct size to electrical load, depends on it.

Heat enters a building in two forms. Sensible heat changes the air temperature. Latent heat is moisture carried in by people, fresh air and infiltration. The total load is the sum of external gains (sun through glass, conduction through walls and roof, infiltration) and internal gains (people, lighting, equipment). It is calculated for the hour and month when the total is highest, called the peak design condition.

Rule-of-thumb sizing, such as a fixed TR per square foot, ignores orientation, glazing, usage and ventilation. It oversizes some rooms and undersizes others. A proper calculation replaces guesswork with room-wise numbers that can be checked and defended.

Our Process

How we do it, step by step

1

Collect inputs

Architectural plans and elevations, wall, roof and glass details, occupancy, lighting and equipment schedules, and operating hours.

2

Set design conditions

Outdoor design temperatures from local climatic data and indoor conditions suited to the use, for example about 24°C and 50 to 55% RH for comfort cooling.

3

Calculate external loads

Conduction through walls, roof and glass, solar gain through glazing allowing for shading, and infiltration through openings.

4

Calculate internal loads

Sensible and latent heat from people, lighting power density, and equipment such as computers, kitchen and process loads.

5

Add the ventilation load

Fresh air required by ASHRAE 62.1 and local code, treated from outdoor to indoor condition. This is often the largest latent component.

6

Consolidate and report

Room-wise sensible, latent and total load in kW and TR, supply airflow, diversity and safety factors, and floor and building summaries.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Methods: ASHRAE Handbook Fundamentals procedures such as CLTD and radiant time series.
  • Outdoor design data: local climatic data for the project city, such as ISHRAE weather data.
  • Indoor design: typically 22 to 25°C and 40 to 60% RH, set by the application.
  • Ventilation: ASHRAE 62.1 rates. For offices this is about 2.5 L/s per person plus 0.3 L/s per m².
  • Units: 1 TR = 3.517 kW = 12,000 BTU/h.
  • Envelope data: U-values of walls and roof, and U-value, SHGC and shading of glass.
  • Diversity and safety factors are applied once and shown openly in the report.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between sensible and latent load?

Sensible load raises air temperature. Latent load is moisture. Both must be removed, and the ratio between them affects coil and equipment selection.

What is a TR?

A ton of refrigeration, equal to 12,000 BTU/h or about 3.517 kW of cooling.

Why not use a rule of thumb?

Loads vary widely with glazing, orientation, occupancy and usage. A single figure per square foot cannot capture that.

When should the calculation be repeated?

When the layout, glazing, occupancy or use of the space changes, or when equipment is added.
