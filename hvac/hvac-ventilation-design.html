Overview

What is Ventilation Design?

Ventilation supplies fresh air and removes stale air, heat, odours, fumes and smoke. In buildings it is both a comfort requirement and a safety requirement.

Different spaces need different solutions. Basements and car parks need extraction of vehicle fumes. Staircases and lift lobbies may need pressurisation so smoke does not enter escape routes. Sewage treatment plants and plant rooms need air changes to control odour and heat. Toilets, kitchens and electrical rooms each have their own requirement.

Design begins with the air-change rate or airflow required by code and use, followed by fan selection, duct or jet-fan layout, make-up air provision and control, including fire-mode operation where required.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Basement and car-park ventilation: normal-mode air changes plus a higher smoke-extraction rate, as set by NBC and the local fire authority.
  • Jet-fan systems move air along the car park without long ducts, and can be zoned by CO sensors.
  • Staircase pressurisation: a pressure difference across closed doors of the order of 50 Pa, with enough airflow to keep smoke out when doors open, as per applicable code.
  • Pressure relief provision so doors stay operable when the fan is running.
  • STP and wet areas: air changes for odour, with corrosion-resistant fans and ducts.
  • Plant and electrical rooms: ventilation sized to limit temperature rise.
  • Fire-rated fans, wiring and dampers where smoke control is required.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a jet-fan system?

Fans mounted at the ceiling push air along a basement toward extraction points, avoiding extensive ductwork.

Why pressurise a staircase?

To keep smoke out of the escape route during a fire.

How many air changes are needed?

It depends on the space and local code. We apply the requirement for each area.

Is natural ventilation acceptable?

In some spaces and codes, yes. We check the code and recommend mechanical systems where required.
