Overview

What is Ventilation Design?

Ventilation supplies fresh air and removes stale air, heat, odours, fumes and smoke. In buildings it is both a comfort requirement and a safety requirement.

Different spaces need different solutions. Basements and car parks need extraction of vehicle fumes. Staircases and lift lobbies may need pressurisation so that smoke does not enter escape routes. Sewage treatment plants and plant rooms need ventilation to control odour and heat.

We design each system to suit the space, its use and the applicable code.

Technical Details

Design Considerations

  • Basement and car park ventilation, including smoke extraction as required by the fire authority.
  • Jet-fan systems that move air without long ducts and can be zoned with CO sensors.
  • Staircase pressurisation that keeps smoke out of escape routes.
  • Relief provision so doors stay easy to open while fans are running.
  • Corrosion-resistant fans and ducts in STP and wet areas.
  • Plant and electrical rooms ventilated to limit temperature rise.
  • Fire-rated fans, wiring and dampers where smoke control is required.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a jet-fan system?

Fans mounted at the ceiling push air along a basement towards extraction points, avoiding extensive ductwork.

Why pressurise a staircase?

To keep smoke out of the escape route during a fire.

How much airflow is needed?

It depends on the space and the local code. We apply the requirement for each area.

Is natural ventilation acceptable?

In some spaces and under some codes, yes. We check the code and recommend mechanical systems where required.
