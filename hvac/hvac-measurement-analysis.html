Overview

What is Measurement Analysis?

Measurement analysis, often called testing and balancing, checks that an installed HVAC system delivers what was designed. Equipment may be installed correctly yet still deliver the wrong airflow to rooms.

We measure airflow at terminals and ducts, adjust dampers so each room receives its design airflow, record temperatures and humidity, and compare results with design values.

The same process is used to audit existing systems that have comfort complaints or high energy use.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Method: duct traverse readings and terminal readings, following ASHRAE and standard testing and balancing practice.
  • Tolerance: measured airflow is generally accepted within about ±10% of design, unless specified otherwise.
  • Instruments are calibrated.
  • System must be complete, with clean filters and open fire dampers, before balancing.
  • Total airflow checked at the fan and compared with the sum of terminals.
  • Temperature readings taken at a grid of points at working height.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When should balancing be done?

After installation and before handover, or when comfort complaints arise.

Can you audit an existing system?

Yes.

What does CFM mean?

Cubic feet per minute, a measure of airflow. It is also expressed in L/s or m³/h.

Does balancing reduce energy use?

Often yes, because fans no longer need to over-deliver.
