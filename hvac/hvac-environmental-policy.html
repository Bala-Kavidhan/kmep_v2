Overview

What is Environmental Policy?

HVAC affects the environment in two main ways: the energy buildings use for cooling, and the refrigerants that can leak. Both matter.

Refrigerants differ in Global Warming Potential (GWP) and, for older types, Ozone Depletion Potential. Older refrigerants such as R22 are being phased out under the Montreal Protocol. Newer lower-GWP options such as R32 and, in some applications, R290 or HFOs are replacing them.

Our policy is to design and install efficient systems, choose lower-impact refrigerants where suitable, prevent leaks, recover refrigerant when servicing, and manage site waste responsibly.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Select equipment with high part-load efficiency and good star ratings.
  • Follow applicable ozone-depleting substances and refrigerant handling rules.
  • Only trained persons handle refrigerant. Use recovery machines.
  • Nitrogen pressure and vacuum tests to minimise leak risk.
  • Segregate packaging, metal, insulation and oils for reuse or authorised disposal.
  • Water treatment and efficient operation of cooling towers.

Refrigerants at a glance

RefrigerantNotes
R22Contains chlorine and depletes ozone. Being phased out. Not recommended for new systems.
R410ACommon in VRF and splits. No ozone depletion but high GWP.
R32Lower GWP than R410A and efficient. Mildly flammable class, so safety rules apply.
R134aCommon in chillers. Moderate GWP.
R290 / HFOsVery low GWP options for suitable applications. Flammability and charge limits must be managed.
