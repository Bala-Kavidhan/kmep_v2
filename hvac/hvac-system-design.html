Overview

What is HVAC System Design?

System design is choosing and engineering the complete air-conditioning arrangement for a building. It decides how cooling is produced, how it is delivered and how it is controlled, and it sets the capital cost and running cost for decades.

No single system suits every building. Split and ducted units suit small spaces, VRV/VRF suits multi-zone buildings with varied loads, packaged units suit large open spaces, and chilled water plants suit large campuses and critical facilities. The best choice depends on load size and pattern, space available, budget, reliability needs and maintenance capability.

We compare options on capital cost, energy use and maintenance, then engineer the chosen system in full: equipment selection, refrigerant or water circuits, schematics, controls and integration with ventilation and the building management system.

Technical Details

Parameters, standards and practice

  • Efficiency metrics: COP, EER, IPLV and ISEER, compared at part load and not only at full load.
  • Chilled water: commonly about 7°C supply and 12°C return, or a higher temperature difference for lower pumping energy.
  • Condenser water for water-cooled plants: about 32°C in and 37°C out, set by climate.
  • Refrigerants: choose by efficiency, safety and regulation. See the Environmental Policy page.
  • Redundancy: standby capacity for critical areas, for example N+1 chillers or pumps.
  • Energy code: alignment with ECBC and local energy requirements where applicable.
  • Fresh air and exhaust balanced to keep spaces slightly positive.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

VRV or chiller: how do we choose?

Mainly by load size, usage pattern, space and lifecycle cost. VRV suits mid-size multi-zone buildings. Chillers suit large loads.

What is part-load efficiency?

Most systems run below full capacity most of the time, so efficiency at part load drives annual energy use.

Can an existing system be redesigned?

Yes. We audit it and propose upgrades or replacement.

Do you design controls?

Yes, including scheduling, interlocks and BMS integration.
